## Original rarity model

This is an editorial familiarity estimate, **not a measurement of player choices** and not Krillion's private scoring system. For each canonical answer, English phrase frequency from wordfreq 3.1.1 provides a baseline: Zipf frequency ≥5 → 10 points; ≥4.1 → 30; ≥3.1 → 60; otherwise 85. The expansion also uses recorded source prominence where available: `1.4 + 1.05 × log10(popularity + 1)` is an additional familiarity floor; popularity is the recorded source measure, such as Wikidata sitelinks or city population. It is not a count of players. Explicit common/familiar/known overrides calibrate category-specific names (for example Labrador Retriever, Quartz, Python and Einstein). Regular word inflections inherit the familiarity of their dictionary base where identifiable, so adding an ordinary plural or past-tense ending is not a rarity loophole. Selected memorable discoveries receive 100; otherwise a low-familiarity candidate is chosen deterministically. Every category has at least one such discovery. Values are generated once and shipped as static data; there is no AI API call or player tracking while playing. Original packs keep their existing ranking and supplied score allocations.

The 47 broad banks use phrase-frequency tiers (5 / 4.1 / 3.1 cutoffs), explicit common-name overrides, recorded prominence thresholds of 50 and 10 where available, and selected 100-point discoveries. Their generator records each category’s source and ranking version. The generators record all editorial overrides. Canonical names and curated aliases take priority; duplicate aliases are merged or removed where ambiguous. Numerical asteroid IDs alone are not accepted. The matcher never synthesizes initialisms. Only canonical names and explicit aliases can be exact matches, including recorded real abbreviations and symbols. Short acronym aliases are exact-only. For corrections it checks weighted typing edits separately for each content word, treats harmless function words and explicit pack context consistently, and rejects unrelated extra words. Omitting one content word requires at least two strong identifying anchors, high coverage and one clear completion. Single shared nouns, ambiguous omissions and sequel-number changes are rejected. Every fuzzy proposal appears only after submission and requires another submit to confirm; nothing is suggested while typing. Ties among complete spelling candidates favor proximity, then familiarity and alphabetical order. See ANSWER-MATCHING.md. This is spelling correction over recorded inventories, not semantic verification of arbitrary new answers, and mistakes remain possible. No points are reduced for corrected input.

The separate seven-question percentile curve remains a disclosed estimate centered near 265 points. Its parameters do not represent collected scores and are not automatically fitted to these banks.
