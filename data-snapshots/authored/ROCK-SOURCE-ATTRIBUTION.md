# Rock-type inventory

This is one broad question, with 193 answer identities. It does not produce a
question per colour, location, age or mineral percentage.

The factual named-rock list comes from the English Wikipedia
[List of rock types](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_rock_types), retrieved
October 25, 2026. Credit its contributors and linked page history under
[CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/). The derived
name inventory retains this attribution/license. Explanatory label-cell notes
are removed from answers; images and article descriptions are not game text.

Primary classification checks:

- [USGS: rock and mineral distinction](https://www.usgs.gov/faqs/what-difference-between-a-rock-and-a-mineral)
- [National Park Service: rock classification](https://www.nps.gov/subjects/geology/how-rocks-are-classified.htm)
- [National Park Service: igneous rocks](https://www.nps.gov/subjects/geology/igneous.htm)
- [British Geological Survey: rocks and minerals](https://www.bgs.ac.uk/discovering-geology/rocks-and-minerals/)

The independent common-input sweep includes the elementary igneous, sedimentary
and metamorphic families as well as familiar named rocks. Intrusive/plutonic,
extrusive/volcanic, diabase/dolerite and other established synonyms share their
appropriate identities. A'a/Aa is an actual short Hawaiian rock name, not an
invented abbreviation. The uncertain wad/mineraloid and idealised pyrolite
entries are held separately. Pure mineral/element names, concrete and mountain
names are not silently substituted for rock types.
