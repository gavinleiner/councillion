# Broad food inventory sources

Four whole food concepts were admitted in development on 2026-10-26. The leaf-wrapped food candidate remains excluded from normal play pending its separate article reconciliation. No source list section or ingredient combination creates another question.

The factual names in `sources/food-completion-lists.json.gz` were extracted from the full published Wikipedia lists of maize dishes, custard desserts and pickled foods. Original URLs, capture dates and SHA-256 receipts accompany the source data. Wikipedia material is available under CC BY-SA; see https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Copyrights and each linked article history for contributors. Original narrative prose and images are not included in the gameplay banks. Adaptations merge equivalent names, add explicitly documented common foods and exclude out-of-scope source rows. The row decisions are recorded in `food-completion-holds.json`.

Ice-cream topping and mix-in repairs use the named Cold Stone ingredient statement and create-your-own menu, Culver's published menu, Dairy Queen treats and the Mars Foodservices Skittles recipe. These document ingredients and historical product preparations, not a promise of availability at every location today. Recipe-author evidence supports ordinary Skittles toppings. Published nutrition listings additionally document Twix, Whoppers and Nilla wafer mix-ins.

Nanaimo bars and custard creams use named recipes from Kraft Heinz, King Arthur Baking and The Great British Bake Off to establish their custard-powder ingredient. The custard scope states that powder-based preparations count; merely containing whipped cream does not. Primary preservation and recipe sources supplement the pickle and corn lists. Exact supporting URLs remain on every question in `batches/01-concrete-situations.json` and the public manifest.

Synonyms share one scoring identity, including custard bao/bun, Portuguese egg tart/pastel de nata, creme renversee/flan and tamal/tamale. Common-input checks, source holds and explicit wrong-answer boundaries are part of the audit rather than claims that a changing food vocabulary can be mathematically exhaustive.

Points are editorial familiarity estimates within each question, not measured player frequencies.
