# Fictional intelligence and covert operatives

Batch 39 adds **one question**, “Name a spy or secret agent from fiction.”
Its 318 canonical characters and 34 unchanged independent familiar inputs are
answers inside that single subject. Source rows, aliases, fictional agencies,
media titles and preliminary scientist/doctor research are not more questions.

## Sources and role review

The complete public indexed body of Wikipedia's list of fictional secret agents
was read with section and line context. The list itself warns about missing
citations, and an occurrence is not independent primary verification. Forty-two
source leads are held: ordinary police/detective-only entries, series and group
names, a mascot, unsupported roles and a source name error. Unknown characters
are not called fake merely because they are unfamiliar. Original creator,
publisher and authorized distributor material supplements recognizable names
and actual identities. The receipts distinguish indexed reads, actual HTML
captures, unavailable pages, JavaScript-only shells and an age/country gate.
No login, age-gate submission, external browser automation or privacy workaround
was used. A failed request does not substantiate a character.

Relevant evidence includes Ian Fleming's characters, Ubisoft's Splinter Cell
brand record, IOI's cast, Konami's original and remake character material,
Marvel's published guides and comic-series records, Disney/D23, Crunchyroll's
licensed Spy x Family guide, Miramax's Spy Kids pages, the studios' Kingsman,
Mission: Impossible, Jason Bourne and Johnny English material, FX's Archer,
AFI's film cast record, Warner Bros.' Get Smart marketing PDF, the authorized
Warner Bros. Chuck synopsis on Apple TV, John le Carré's book page and the
original public-domain His Last Bow text on Project Gutenberg. Individual
URLs and which public character/identity fact each supports are frozen beside
the reviewed rows. Only names and reviewed facts are shipped, not source prose,
artwork, scripts or game assets. The unavailable NBC page was replaced by the
actual authorized Apple TV synopsis as the source pointer for Chuck's trio.

Documented private/covert support operatives count; formal government employment
is not required. Kim Possible and Ron Stoppable are included on that basis.
A secret identity alone does not qualify every superhero. Sherlock Holmes,
Watson and Von Bork have actual espionage roles in His Last Bow; this is not
blanket approval of every detective. Generic agency membership and film cast
membership do not approve all names in a surrounding table or navigation bar.

## Identity and grading

Solid Snake, Big Boss/Naked Snake and Venom Snake remain three characters.
Gary Unwin/Eggsy and Harry Hart/Galahad remain different people despite a
transferred codename. Natasha Romanoff/Black Widow is separate from Natasha
Fatale. The two Sams, the different Six characters, and Get Smart's Agent 13
versus Sharon Carter use qualifiers where needed. Nick Fury's article discusses
several officeholders; that is not a reason to merge their full names.

Winter Soldier/Bucky Barnes, Skye/Quake/Daisy Johnson, Perry/Agent P and actual
published 006/007 identities have explicit evidence. M, Q and Team Fortress 2's
Spy are real public character names, not generated initials. JB and NP are not
invented shortcuts. The existing `preserveNumericTokens` option is enabled only
for this pack so 0.07 and Agent 4.7 cannot become 007 and Agent 47 by stripping
punctuation. No global matching or selection algorithm changes were made.

Rarity tiers are deliberate editorial estimates, not measured player frequencies
or spelling length. Common James Bond/Ethan Hunt answers are entry-tier; the
documented Alec Leamas is the rare anchor. Correct names share character identity
and cannot score twice through aliases.

## Validation and limits

All 345 unit tests and the production build pass at development count 474.
The build checks 8,969 independent familiar inputs and 966 wrong boundaries.
Local actual gameplay passes 41 cases: 27 exact names/scores, 11 rejections and
three corrections that appear only after submission. These include malformed
numeric codes, unrelated real actors, distinct Snake characters and the Marvel
public aliases. Puffer checks distinct-character scoring and duplicate aliases.
General local browser checks pass saved unseen-queue migration, persistence,
independent shared Daily contexts, question library/sample and laptop/mobile
overflow. All external browser requests were blocked.

These checks support the reviewed inventory; they do not prove that every
fictional covert operative ever published has been enumerated. The normal-play
admission is explicit and separate from inventory generation. The full 5,000
question release remains unfinished, and production is not updated by this
development checkpoint.
