# Cooking techniques and culinary nuts/edible seeds

Two whole familiar culinary categories were selected and reviewed October 27, 2026. The complete retained 186-row cooking-technique list and 100-row culinary-nut extraction are in sources/food-basics-list-source.json.gz. They derive from Wikipedia's List of cooking techniques and List of culinary nuts under CC BY-SA; source meanings are retained for inspection and not displayed as copied instructional prose in the game. The per-row holds and canonical mappings are in food-basics-review-ledger.json.

List membership does not automatically make an answer valid. The cooking review removes utensils, finished dishes, ingredients, malformed entries, philosophical models and source headings. Familiar missing actions such as kneading, air frying, chopping, dicing, slicing, peeling, mixing, folding and sifting are explicit additions, supported by independent familiar probes and the primary King Arthur Baking and Redpath terminology resources linked per question. Real public verb/noun forms share identity. Infusion/steeping, peeling tomatoes/peeling and bain-marie/double boiling do not score twice. Incorrect source text such as maliard is not invented as an exact alias; normal spelling correction remains unchanged.

The nut/seed prompt explicitly includes culinary nuts, grains, pulses and edible spice seeds. It therefore accepts rice, wheat, maize, chickpea, lentil, pumpkin seed, sunflower seed, sesame and many other ordinary seeds absent from a culinary-nuts-only source. FAO's Human Nutrition in the Developing World and Compendium of Forgotten Foods support the broad food-seed scope. Culinary tuber names such as tiger nut are covered by the culinary-nut wording; botanical classification headings and books are excluded. Genuine common names merge, including filbert/hazelnut, garbanzo/chickpea, linseed/flaxseed and pepita/pumpkin seed. Water chestnut refers to the actual culinary Trapa water-caltrop nut here; the water-chestnut corm is not silently represented as a seed.

The two current inventories contain 186 and 174 canonical names respectively, with 60 and 68 independently specified familiar probes. They count as two questions. Scores are editorial familiarity estimates, not actual observed player frequencies. The source labels, primary reference URLs and semantic holds remain inspectable.
