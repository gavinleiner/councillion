# Whole NFL franchise roster inventories

Prepared October 27, 2026. Thirty-two real franchise career rosters are retained as separate authored candidates. Seven enter ordinary play at this checkpoint: Bills, Panthers, Cowboys, Chiefs, Patriots, Eagles and 49ers. The other twenty-five remain outside the ordinary pool while other subjects expand; their names and source-row counts do not add to the playable question count. No season, position or city-name subset produces additional questions.

Source data is nflverse's complete retained annual-roster facts for 1920–2026, available from https://github.com/nflverse/nflverse-data/releases/tag/rosters . The source loader and field semantics are documented at https://nflreadr.nflverse.com/reference/load_rosters.html and https://nflreadr.nflverse.com/articles/dictionary_rosters.html . The retained archive has 142,739 rows, including one unnamed record that is not an answer. Names, team codes, seasons, positions and source player IDs are retained in sources/nfl-franchise-rosters.json.gz. Public full-name fields from the matching nflverse player table are retained in sources/nfl-public-names.json.gz; no birth dates, contact fields, headshots or other biographical columns are redistributed. nflverse data is attributed under CC BY 4.0 as specified by https://github.com/nflverse . This roster data is not the separately licensed FTN charting dataset.

A raw city/team abbreviation is not a franchise identity. The explicit code/season rules in nfl-franchise-specifications.json use the Pro Football Hall of Fame's franchise history: https://www.profootballhof.com/football-history/national-football-league-franchise-histories . Examples: pre-1960 BUF is a different team from the modern Bills; CLE1937–1945 is the Rams while CLE1946 onward is the Browns; STL1960–1987 is Cardinals while STL1995–2001 and SL2002–2015 are Rams; BAL1953–1983 is the Colts while BAL1996 onward is Ravens. The earlier 1947–1950 Colts are a distinct defunct franchise. Houston Oilers/Titans are separate from the 2002 Texans. Boston, Dallas, New York, Minnesota, Miami, Detroit, Los Angeles and Chicago code reuse are also explicitly reviewed. The joint wartime Steagles/Card-Pitt rosters belong to both represented franchises. Draft rights and coaches alone are not roster membership; the stated roster scope does include offseason/practice squads rather than claiming regular-season appearances.

Stable GSIS/PFR identifiers establish actual personal-name changes. Matching text alone must not join the identities of different people called Steve Smith, Clay Matthews, Patrick Surtain or Michael Pittman. Historical rows without player identifiers retain their exact source names. Known public aliases such as Steve Smith Sr., Josh Cribbs, Ed “Too Tall” Jones, Billy “White Shoes” Johnson, Elroy Hirsch and personal suffixes are explicitly scoped to the correct person/franchise. Source-profile full display names, actual first/last name fields and source football names expand real full-name coverage. The publisher short_name field and generated initial/surname shortcuts are excluded. Public aliases that genuinely name two eligible people in a single roster choose the independently familiar player, then longer franchise tenure; the eligible owners and choice remain in the review ledger. Identical submitted labels can score once, even where two different people share that label.

There are 639 independent familiar-player probes across the 32 proposed subjects. Every common input and rare anchor is checked against the retained roster, and explicit wrong-franchise boundaries cover reused codes, unrelated teams and name collisions. Counts, historical season coverage and shared-name decisions are in nfl-franchise-checks.json; exact public-name provenance is in nfl-public-name-proof.json. A source archive's size does not certify factual completeness. The snapshot's boundary remains explicit, so later roster changes require fresh data.

Points are curated familiarity estimates: independently recognizable examples occupy the lower tiers, established franchise players use ordinary tiers, less familiar names occupy deep tiers, and one deliberately chosen real lesser-known roster member is the top-tier anchor. These are not measured answer-frequency statistics. The global spelling matcher is unchanged.
