# Counsel Clash and Final Figure

Current release `20261105-figure-curated-v12` includes the rebuilt team court and a new 1,001-question Final Figure bank. Both modes use Google-authenticated rooms alongside Puffer. Existing Puffer rating, Daily, Practice, moderation, characters and the 499-question ordinary pool are unchanged.

## Counsel Clash

Two teams, one to five ready players each. The two benches are Petitioners and Respondents. Representatives and answering turns rotate. A face-off has two buzzers, one answer from each side unless the first answer is tied for highest, then the winner chooses play/pass. Equal-valued answers preserve first-buzzer priority. Three misses or timeouts open one opposing-team steal; the steal transfers the bank without adding the stealing answer's value.

The compact online format uses four boards with 1×, 1×, 2× and 3× scoring. Reach 300 earlier or lead after four; a tie adds another triple board. This is a stated online adaptation, not a promise of an identical television episode format. The winning team chooses two Fast Money runners: five unused boards, 20 seconds then 25 seconds, a combined 200-point goal. A solo winning team gets one run and a 100-point goal. Drafts autosave privately and are banked at the timer. The second runner receives a retry for a duplicated canonical answer; the first runner's responses stay hidden from the second runner until both finish. The audience sees the first column during the second run.

200 individually selected aired boards, 1,255 displayed answer categories, from seasons 16–25. These are **fan transcriptions of broadcasts**, not official raw survey data or an affiliated product. Prompts and labels are condensed; published point values and combined answer categories are retained. Totals range from 52 to 100; they are deliberately not normalized to 100. Only displayed board categories score, as in a survey-board game. Familiar equivalents and spelling variations are accepted through explicit aliases and the existing conservative matcher.

The complete source URLs, source page hashes, board indexes and point vectors are in [survey-review.json](data-snapshots/court-games/survey-review.json). The additional 160 boards and their source indexes, original categories and score vectors are recorded in [library-expansion-review.json](data-snapshots/court-games/library-expansion-review.json); large source-page archives stay outside the shipped repository. Each completed board links its source; Fast Money results also link their source boards. Selection favors approachable food, daily situations, movies, sports, wordplay and animals. No claim is made that the questions themselves have a measured popularity ordering.

Full-episode reference: [official Family Feud — Steve Harvey’s first full game, 19:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-TKBXleCEUU), watched from the opening through the finale. Notes distinguish the television bank-only steal from live-stage scoring. The face-off uses original buzzer order if both initial representatives miss; a repeated revealed answer during control requests another answer without spending a turn. A short referee-controlled “survey says” beat separates submissions from judgement. Only the defending bench may confer privately, including while preparing a steal.

Rules references: [Family Feud Live rules](https://familyfeudlive.com/Family_Feud_Rules_Spring.pdf), [Hasbro home-game instructions](https://www.hasbro.com/common/instruct/familyfe.pdf), and the [licensed Endless Games booklet](https://content.cmpl.org/bg/FamilyFeud.pdf). These establish the face-off, board, strike/steal and bonus-round structure; the compact online format is described above.

## Final Figure

Two to eight ready players. The default is six 30-second rounds, with six different subjects drawn from Gaming, Sports, Screen, Music, World, Nature and Everyday. Hosts can choose longer matches. Each room draws afresh from the complete **1,001-question admitted bank**; previous players and previous rooms do not constrain the draw. Every complete seven-question subject cycle contains each category once. A ten-question topic cooldown avoids franchise clusters; rolling six-question windows limit calendar questions to two and straightforward warmups to one. Repeated runtime, episode-count, track-count and age forms also have a cooldown. Small exhausted banks have an explicit fallback, but actual-bank tests verify the opening thirty questions across 400 seeds.

The pool has 134 Gaming, 171 Sports, 189 Screen, 113 Music, 191 World, 104 Nature and 99 Everyday questions. Subject cycles make actual play balanced even though the source-reviewed pool has different category sizes. No subject is over 20% of the pool. Broad game mechanics, recognizable stories, records, animals, everyday objects and historical milestones replace the previous technical bank. No combinations of Steam tags, title filters, source entities or unverified drafts are counted as questions.

One estimate per player per round. Commas, decimals, scientific notation and k/m/b suffixes work; negative values, non-finite values, units and values above 10^15 are rejected. Accuracy is `round(1000 * exp(-2.2 * abs(guess-target) / max(abs(target), scale)))`, clamped to 0–1000. Calendar questions instead use a fixed 50-year denominator: guessing twenty years away is meaningfully different from guessing the exact year. Closest estimates earn another 200; equal distance shares the bonus. Missing answers get zero. Highest match total wins, with shared winners possible. Year displays omit thousands separators and retain fractional estimates. The reveal ruler uses the calendar span rather than treating the four-digit year as its scale.

Only the selected six to ten question IDs travel with room state, instead of sending the whole bank on room updates. Targets, sources, explanations and scoring spans enter public match state at the reveal. Each reveal shows the answer, scope explanation, linked source, guesses, points and comparison line. This state privacy does not change the casual host-authoritative security limits described below.

[figure-curated-v12.json](data-snapshots/court-games/figure-curated-v12.json) contains all 1,001 original question records and individual factual reviews. Each record has a content hash plus an actual source-page hash, web-reader evidence hash, or an explicitly identified prior published review. Reused reviews are not described as fresh fetches. Editorial checks reject ambiguous measurements, unsupported quantities, duplicate facts, giveaways and narrow filler. For example, the conflicting 124/125-minute Spirited Away runtime was withheld. Full third-party source archives remain outside the shipped repository. `node scripts/build-figure-library.mjs --check` verifies every review matches its exact admitted question and the generated runtime file.

## Visual direction

The published court rebuild uses one cohesive painted underwater pixel court, five permanent podium positions per team, and a matching foreground layer that places the cast behind their shared benches. Players keep their own positions between the waiting room and play; all ten remain visible. The rebuilt Feud menu and both waiting rooms show the current cast, ready states, invites and settings. Background life, host bubbles and opponent-only Space pebble tosses keep the waiting court active. Feud has explicit briefing readiness, a question-reading phase, clear actor guidance, post-Enter private spelling confirmation, and timed reveal/handoff pauses before the next answer clock begins. Figure keeps its ordinary question presentation during answering and its animated evidence scroll for reveals.

Fast Money now presents a single question at a time. Enter submits the current answer, Pass returns to that question after the first lap if there is time, and drafts save privately. The second runner receives an immediate duplicate-category retry without seeing their teammate's answer. The first 20-second run is followed by a 12-second survey reveal, privately delivered to the audience while the second runner sees only a quiet-bench screen. Each runner gets instructions and a ready button, then the first question is read before the full 20/25-second timer starts. During run two, the audience retains the first answer column while the second runner cannot access it. The final review keeps the first column and its total in place, reveals each second answer before its points, and celebrates only when the displayed score reaches 200. Answers beyond that winning reveal are not needed or added to the final score. A solo winner has one 100-point run. The same protected response rules and new-host recovery apply; no answer categories are made public to the isolated runner.

Final Figure enters through an open-water hub with a personal diver, depth rail, seal animation, character picker and live expedition list. Its play screen is an open-water expedition using the same continuous ocean renderer as Daily, with private numerical estimates. Each accuracy point becomes one metre of depth (including the 200-metre closest bonus); the original scoring formula is unchanged. The camera follows the local diver, or the leader for spectators, through sunlight, twilight, midnight ruins, abyss and hadal scenery. The reveal compares guesses, awards depth, and lets players open the source explanation. Six-round depth totals determine the winner. Input and caret remain stable when other crew members seal estimates.

Clash's existing Puffer scenery is reused deliberately to match the requested style; the experimental court, studio and coral backgrounds are not shipped. The earlier generated chart-room image is retained as an unused art asset; both current hubs use existing approved Puffer/Daily scenery. No new raster art was generated for this update. The chart-room image was generated with built-in image generation and encoded losslessly as WebP. Its saved file and exact prompt are in [ART-NOTES.md](assets/court-games/ART-NOTES.md) and [generation-prompts-v2.json](assets/court-games/generation-prompts-v2.json). The new slate bench is a native pixel SVG. There is no runtime generation or API dependency. Ambient canvas motion, character idles, seals, reveals and camera transitions respect reduced motion. Leaving the expedition destroys its animation frame, resize listener and visibility listener.

## Rooms, reconnects and privacy

New games use the existing server-enforced Google room authorization. No database rules are loosened or new allowlist added. They have distinct `catalogBuild` values under `rooms/` and `directory/`, so they do not appear in ordinary Puffer's room list or each other's mode list. Clients write only their own member/request nodes. The host owns public state and private response writes. Huddles are copied only to teammates' protected response nodes; estimates and Fast Money drafts/results live in each player's protected secret response until reveal. Sealed estimates are immutable at the referee.

Accepted state, acknowledgement and private result are written atomically. Duplicate request IDs are suppressed, including an already-acknowledged request seen by a new host. Reactions cannot overwrite an in-flight guess. A deliberate host exit transfers to another online participant immediately; abrupt disconnections use the existing 30-second host-claim authorization. New hosts rehydrate accepted private estimates and Fast Money drafts before continuing. Briefly offline seats retain their reconnect grace, while available teammates get new turns. Late arrivals spectate until a rematch. An over-capacity waiting-room join is rejected clearly and its unused membership removed. A whole team leaving ends the match.

This is host-authoritative casual play, like the existing Puffer architecture: an intentionally malicious host or a modified client can inspect bundled question facts. It is not a secure competitive server or an anti-cheat guarantee. The new modes have match scores, not Puffer ranked rating.

## Historical court library expansion — 20261105-court-library-v10

Adds 160 selected Feud boards and 168 numerical questions. The shipped banks now contain 200 survey boards (1,255 scored categories) and 210 numerical questions, with 30 in each of seven subjects. New Figure subjects include familiar games, music and films, food packaging, sports records, animals, science and landmarks. Prompts specify the relevant edition, measurement scope and rounding. Survey scores remain historical board scores; explicit synonyms cover missing-word prompts and normal everyday wording without generated initials.

The source snapshot is [library-expansion.json](data-snapshots/court-games/library-expansion.json). `node scripts/build-court-library.mjs --check` verifies the deterministic runtime output. `node scripts/audit-court-library.mjs` checks all 200 source score vectors and category counts, all 3,775 canonical/alias round trips, numerical review contracts, input magnitudes, category balance, 800 fresh match draws and 800 attainable Fast Money draws. Normal round selection still uses the entire shuffled bank with no cross-lobby player history.

Fast Money retains its fresh unused draw unless the theoretical sum of perfect answers cannot reach 100 for a solo runner or 200 for two. In that case it replaces the weakest board from the remaining shuffle until the target is reachable. No source scores are changed. Adversarial tests force both impossible cases as well as checking random matches.

The first Figure prompt now chooses ink from the actual camera waterline, rather than score: the intro can already be underwater while depth is zero. `node tests/court-games-opening-browser.mjs` follows lobby → intro → first estimate at four viewport sizes and checks contrast against rendered canvas pixels, then checks the surface at the same zero score. Live questions remain plain text; the scroll is reserved for the reveal. Existing room builds receive the normal refresh boundary because the admitted question IDs have expanded.
